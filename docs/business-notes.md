# Business notes

## Who it is for
* **Passengers** who speak Hindi or Punjabi first, often seniors on a first trip abroad, who will not press a call button for a small need.
* **Cabin crew** who need to understand a request quickly and be sure an announcement landed.
* **The airline**, which wants fewer unmet requests, fewer repeated safety explanations and a record that exit-row checks were understood.

## Context
Indian visitors to Japan reached about 315,100 in 2025 (JNTO), up 35% on 2024. JAL flies three non-stop routes between Japan and India (Haneda and Narita to Delhi, Narita to Bengaluru). Cruise noise in a jet cabin is typically 80 to 85 dB(A) while conversation is about 60 dB (Canadian Acoustical Association journal).

## Why it fits an airline
* No new hardware: passenger phones, seatback screens or a tablet, plus a crew tablet.
* Works on a cabin-local network with no internet.
* Two taps for crew. The crew stay in charge and announcements are unchanged; HASHI only adds confirmation.
* Consent based, nothing stored beyond the flight.

## What we would measure
Time from request to "understood"; requests that go unmet; announcements confirmed; exit-row checks passed first time; senior passenger satisfaction.

## Roadmap
1. **Hackathon MVP:** Hindi and Punjabi, a Delhi to Tokyo scenario.
2. **Pilot-ready:** one India route, crew feedback, safety review, real cabin recordings for the matcher.
3. **Scale:** Tamil, Kannada, Thai, Vietnamese; Japanese passengers abroad; crew routing by language; links to reservation and meal systems.
4. **Beyond the cabin:** airports, hospitals, hotels.

## Open questions for a real deployment
Airline security approval for a local server, how the QR is handed out at check-in, who owns the translation review, and how the exit-row procedure maps onto the airline's own rules.
